Improving the Dissolution of Compound X Through Amorphous Solid Dispersions Prepared by Hot Melt Extrusion and Spray Drying

Abstract
Compound X is a poorly water soluble weak base. Amorphous solid dispersions were prepared with Kollidon VA64 and HPMCAS-MG by hot melt extrusion and by spray drying. All extrudates were amorphous by XRPD. The copovidone dispersion at 20% drug load showed a fourfold increase in dissolution relative to crystalline drug.

2. Materials and Methods
2.1 Materials
Compound X was supplied by the sponsor. Kollidon VA64 was obtained from BASF. HPMCAS-MG (AQOAT AS-MG) was obtained from Shin-Etsu. Vitamin E TPGS and Lutrol F68 were used as surfactants.

2.2 Hot melt extrusion
Formulation F1 contained Compound X and Kollidon VA64 at a drug load of 20% w/w. Formulation F2 contained Compound X, Kollidon VA64 and 5% Vitamin E TPGS at a drug load of 30% w/w. Extrusion was performed on a 16 mm twin screw extruder at a screw speed of 100 rpm with a maximum barrel temperature of 150 °C.

[PAGE 2]
2.3 Spray drying
Formulation F3 was prepared by dissolving Compound X and HPMCAS-MG at a ratio of 1:3 in acetone and spray drying with an inlet temperature of 85 °C. Formulation F4 contained Compound X, HPMCAS-MG and 2% Lutrol F68 at a drug load of 25% w/w and was spray dried under the same conditions.

2.4 Characterization
Solid state was assessed by XRPD and modulated DSC. The glass transition temperature of F1 was 98 °C and of F3 was 112 °C.

2.5 Stability
Samples were stored open at 40 °C / 75% RH for 3 months. F4 was additionally stored at 25 °C / 60% RH.

[PAGE 3]
3. Results
F1 and F2 were fully amorphous by XRPD after extrusion. F3 was amorphous by XRPD. F4 showed small crystalline peaks after spray drying, so it was classed as partially amorphous.
After storage, F1 remained amorphous at 3 months. F2 recrystallized within 4 weeks. F3 remained amorphous. The stability of F4 is discussed in a separate report.
